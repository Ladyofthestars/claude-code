# Facts and sources: "You Are Made of Stars"

Every fact and number in the narration, with where it comes from. Each source was opened and the wording checked on 1 November 2026. Anything without a solid source was cut.

| # | Fact as said in the video | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hydrogen in your body (in every water molecule) came from the Big Bang; NASA: there are no other appreciable sources of hydrogen in the universe. | NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day, "Where Your Elements Came From", 24 Oct 2017 and 8 Jan 2023: https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap171024.html, https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap230108.html |
| 2 | The carbon and the oxygen in your body were made by nuclear fusion inside stars. | Same NASA APOD pages as #1 |
| 3 | Much of the iron in your body was made in supernovae of stars that exploded long ago and far away. | Same NASA APOD pages as #1 |
| 4 | The gold in jewelry was *likely* made in collisions of neutron stars. | Same NASA APOD pages as #1 |
| 5 | Scientists still debate how much heavy elements come from neutron-star collisions and how much from other kinds of exploding stars. | Monash University news on Kobayashi, Karakas & Lugaro (2020): https://www.monash.edu/science/news-events/news/2020/elements-of-surprise-neutron-stars-contribute-little,-but-somethings-making-gold,-research-finds |
| 6 | The universe began about 13.8 billion years ago (NASA WMAP: 13.77 billion years). | NASA WMAP, "How Old is the Universe?": https://wmap.gsfc.nasa.gov/universe/uni_age.html |
| 7 | The universe was too hot for atoms at first and cooled as it expanded. In the first few minutes, only hydrogen, helium and a small amount of lithium formed. The heavier elements formed later, in stars. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 29.3 "The Beginning of the Universe": https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/29-3-the-beginning-of-the-universe |
| 8 | By weight, the most common elements in your body are oxygen, then carbon, then hydrogen, then nitrogen. | OpenStax *Anatomy & Physiology 2e*, 2.1 (Figure 2.2): https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/2-1-elements-and-atoms-the-building-blocks-of-matter |
| 9 | Six elements (carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, sulfur, phosphorus) make up more than 97% of the mass of a human body. | SDSS press release, "The Elements of Life Mapped Across the Milky Way by SDSS/APOGEE", 5 Jan 2017: https://press.sdss.org/the-elements-of-life-mapped-across-the-milky-way-by-sdssapogee/ |
| 10 | In 2017, astronomers measured the elements in more than 150,000 stars across the Milky Way and found those same elements of life. | Same SDSS press release as #9 |
| 11 | Carbon is the backbone of the large molecules of life. | OpenStax *Biology 2e*, 2.3 "Carbon": https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/2-3-carbon |
| 12 | DNA's building blocks contain a nitrogen-bearing base and a phosphate group. | OpenStax *Biology 2e*, 14.2: https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/14-2-dna-structure-and-sequencing |
| 13 | Bone is hard because of crystals made from calcium phosphate and calcium carbonate. | OpenStax *Anatomy & Physiology 2e*, 6.3 "Bone Structure": https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-3-bone-structure |
| 14 | Hemoglobin in red blood cells contains iron and carries oxygen. | OpenStax *Anatomy & Physiology 2e*, 18.3 "Erythrocytes": https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes |
| 15 | Matter in living things is conserved and recycled; carbon cycles through rocks, air, water and living things. | OpenStax *Biology 2e*, 46.3 "Biogeochemical Cycles": https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/46-3-biogeochemical-cycles |
| 16 | The Sun's energy comes from fusing hydrogen into helium; a small part of the mass becomes energy. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 16.2: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/16-2-mass-energy-and-the-theory-of-relativity |
| 17 | The Sun converts about 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second; about 4 million tons of that becomes energy. | Same OpenStax page as #16 |
| 18 | The Sun has been shining for about 4.5 billion years. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 22.4: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/22-4-further-evolution-of-stars |
| 19 | When a Sun-like star's core runs low on hydrogen, the core contracts and heats up, and the outer layers expand (red giant). | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 22.1: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/22-1-evolution-from-the-main-sequence-to-red-giants |
| 20 | At about 100 million degrees (kelvin), three helium nuclei fuse into one carbon nucleus. Two alone make something that falls apart very quickly. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 22.4 (as #18) |
| 21 | A carbon nucleus can be joined by another helium nucleus to make oxygen. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 22.4 (as #18) |
| 22 | A Sun-like star sheds its outer layers into space (planetary nebula). | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 23.1: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/23-1-the-death-of-low-mass-stars |
| 23 | In massive stars, carbon fuses into oxygen, neon and magnesium, then silicon, sulfur and calcium, then iron. Some final stages take only months or days. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 23.2: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/23-2-evolution-of-massive-stars-an-explosive-finish |
| 24 | Fusion up to iron gives off energy; going past iron does not. Silicon to iron is the last step. | Same OpenStax page as #23 |
| 25 | In less than a second, a core of about one solar mass, about the size of Earth, collapses to less than 20 km across. What remains can be a neutron star. | Same OpenStax page as #23 |
| 26 | For a brief time, a supernova may outshine the entire galaxy it appears in. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 23.3: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/23-3-supernova-observations |
| 27 | A sugar-cube-sized piece of neutron star would weigh about 1 billion tons. | NASA Chandra X-ray Observatory, "Science by the Numbers": https://chandra.harvard.edu/20th/images/Science_by_the_numbers.pdf |
| 28 | On 17 August 2017, LIGO (United States) and Virgo (Italy) detected gravitational waves from two merging neutron stars (GW170817), about 130 million light-years away; telescopes then saw its light. | ESO press release eso1733: https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1733/ |
| 29 | That merger scattered heavy elements including gold and platinum into space. | Same ESO press release as #28 ("Merging neutron stars scatter gold and platinum into space") |
| 30 | Our planetary system formed about 4.5 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud (the solar nebula) that flattened into a disk; dust grains stuck together into larger bodies and planets. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 14.3: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/14-3-formation-of-the-solar-system |
| 31 | "Almost every atom in your body is older than the Earth." | Follows from #1, #2, #3, #6, #7 and #30: hydrogen formed in the first minutes; carbon, oxygen and iron in stars long before the solar system formed 4.5 billion years ago. "Almost" covers the tiny share made later, for example by radioactive decay. |
| 32 | Carl Sagan: "We are made of star-stuff." | Carl Sagan, *Cosmos* (Random House, 1980) and the TV series *Cosmos: A Personal Voyage* (1980) |

| 33 | The Sun is a ball of hot gas; about 73% of its mass is hydrogen ("mostly hydrogen"). | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 15.1: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/15-1-the-structure-and-composition-of-the-sun |
| 34 | The space between the stars holds thin gas and dust. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 20.1: https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/20-1-the-interstellar-medium |
| 35 | Dying stars return their elements to space, enriching the gas and dust that form new stars and planets. | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 23.2 (as #23) and 22.4 "Cosmic Recycling" (as #18) |
| 36 | Stars end up with a layered structure "like an onion". | OpenStax *Astronomy 2e*, 22.4 (as #18) |
| 37 | The body's main elements are shown as a ranked list (oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen), not as bars with percentages, because only the order was checked. | OpenStax *Anatomy & Physiology 2e*, 2.1 (as #8) |

**Cut for lack of a solid source:** the share of the body that is water (I couldn't open the USGS page), how long sunlight takes to reach Earth (not needed), and the exact number of atoms in a body.
