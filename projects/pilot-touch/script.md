# Pilot: new format test for "You've Never Actually Touched Anything" (opening 75 seconds)

Goal: test a faster, story-driven format before rebuilding videos 2 and 3. Cold-open hook in the first seconds, three open loops, a true human story with suspense, no dead air, full-screen cinematic paintings with motion, kinetic text and sound effects.

## 1. Narration

### Part 0
> Right now, you think you're touching your phone. You're not. You never have. Not your phone. Not the floor. Not even the hand of the person you love. Not once in your whole life.

> Because every atom in your body is almost entirely empty. Squeeze the empty space out of every person on Earth, and all eight billion of us would fit inside a sugar cube.

> So why doesn't your hand sink into the table? Why does a punch still hurt? And if nothing ever really touches, what happens when someone holds you?

> To find out, we have to go back more than a hundred years, to a dark lab in Manchester. A twenty-year-old student named Ernest Marsden is firing tiny particles at a sheet of gold, thinner than a hair. And every few thousand shots, one of them comes flying straight back.

> His professor, Ernest Rutherford, later said it was like firing a giant artillery shell at a piece of tissue paper, and watching it bounce back and hit you. So what was it hitting?

## 2. Sources
- Atoms mostly empty; nucleus about 100,000 times smaller than the atom: OpenStax Chemistry 2e, 2.3 (video 3 facts #1–2).
- All 8 billion people in less than a sugar cube: calculated from NASA Chandra (a sugar cube of neutron star weighs about a billion tons) and the UN (8 billion people) (video 3 facts #11–13).
- Ernest Marsden, a 20-year-old student at the University of Manchester, with Hans Geiger, under Ernest Rutherford: H. Geiger and E. Marsden, "On a Diffuse Reflection of the α-Particles", Proc. Royal Society A 82 (1909) 495–500 (Marsden listed as "Hatfield Scholar, University of Manchester"); HyperPhysics, "History of Rutherford Experiment" (gsu.edu).
- About 1 in 8,000 alpha particles reflected (the paper's own measurement, with a metal plate) → said as "every few thousand shots".
- Very thin gold foil; most passed through, a very small number bounced almost straight back: OpenStax Chemistry 2e, 2.2.
- Rutherford's comparison (a 15-inch shell fired at tissue paper that comes back and hits you): his 1936 Cambridge lecture, widely quoted; paraphrased, not quoted.
