You have never actually touched anything. Not the floor under your feet, not this screen, not even the hand of someone you love. The atoms of your body have never met the atoms of anything else.

And yet you feel everything. In this video we go inside the atom, find out why you don't fall through the floor, and discover what touch really is, from Rutherford's gold foil to the densest objects in the universe, and what a Stoic born into slavery understood about it almost two thousand years ago.

This is part three of the series. Part one: "Almost Every Atom in You Is Older Than the Earth". Part two: "What Happens to Your Atoms When You Die?"

## How it is built to keep people watching
- **Hook:** an impossible-sounding claim in the first second, then three quick pictures of things you think you touch (the floor, the screen, a loved one's hand), then the promise of what the video will show.
- **Questions:** every chapter is a question and ends on the next one ("So if atoms are mostly empty, why don't we fall right through the floor?").
- **Something new every few seconds**, checked for every frame: self-drawing diagrams, atoms as swarming clouds of glowing electrons, particles flying through gold foil, counting numbers.
- **Same look as video 2:** glowing white drawings on a deep blue page with drifting stardust and gold highlights.
- **Full circle:** it ends where it began, with the hands, and turns the science into the series' Stoic idea: you meet your own picture of the world, and that picture is within your power.
